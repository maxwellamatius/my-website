2.0 Introduction

The body’s wiring, and how it gets hijacked

A

Central Control System

The nervous system regulates homeostatic equilibrium, motor movements, sensory perception, and autonomic functions, coordinating every organ through rapid electrical and chemical signals.

B

Synaptic Transmission

Electrical action potentials convert to chemical signals: neurotransmitters leave the presynaptic terminal by exocytosis, cross the synaptic cleft, and bind postsynaptic receptors.

C

Pharmacological Interference

Exogenous chemicals can modify neurotransmitter release, receptor action, or clearance and reuptake, tilting the balance of the whole system.

Biological takeaway

A drug does not create new signals. It reshapes the three steps every synapse already performs: release, action, and removal.

4.0 Cellular & Synaptic Mechanisms

Release. Action. Removal.

Stimulant

Amphetamines force exocytosis

Amphetamines drive more dopamine and norepinephrine out of the presynaptic terminal, stimulating vesicle release and flooding the cleft with messenger.

Depressant

Ca²⁺ channels are suppressed

Depressants suppress voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, so less calcium enters the terminal and fewer vesicles fuse with the membrane.

Stimulant

Cocaine blocks the DAT

Cocaine blocks the Dopamine Transporter (DAT) reuptake proteins, so dopamine lingers in the cleft and keeps stimulating the postsynaptic cell.

Depressant

Inhibition is prolonged

Enzymatic interference and prolonged GABA action keep the brain’s main inhibitory signal active for longer than normal.

Stimulant

Agonists depolarize

Receptor agonists open channels for Na⁺ influx, depolarizing the postsynaptic membrane toward threshold and making firing more likely.

Depressant

GABA agonists hyperpolarize

GABA-enhancing drugs increase Cl⁻ influx, hyperpolarizing the membrane and pushing it further from threshold.

Biological takeaway

Na⁺ in means closer to firing. Cl⁻ in means further from firing. The ion that moves decides the neuron’s fate.

5.0 Systemic Physiological Effects

From one synapse to the whole body

Cardiovascular & Respiratory

Sympathetic activation
Tachycardia, hypertension, hyperventilation
Brainstem depression
Bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation
Depressants act on parasympathetic pathways and the medulla, which sets heart and breathing rhythm. Severe depression of the medulla is why overdoses can stop breathing.

Neurological & Motor

Hyper-arousal
Insomnia and tremors
Cerebellar ataxia
Delayed reaction time, slurred speech
The cerebellum coordinates smooth movement. When inhibition dominates, timing and balance break down, which is why reaction time lengthens.
